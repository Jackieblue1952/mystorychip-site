Where It Began

The MyStoryChip Story

How one quiet moment became a new way to preserve memories, meaning, and the stories life leaves behind.

Some things deserve more than being left behind.

This idea did not begin in a boardroom. It began with a quiet day, a cemetery, and one question that would not let go.

As I was visiting family and friends at the cemetery, I found myself thinking about the people buried there and all the stories they could tell if they were still here today. I thought about the stories that died with them — the talents, the tricks of the trade, the memories, and the life experiences that will never be told the same way again.

One headstone especially stayed with me. Frank Sutton. His marker had a few small mementos of who he was, but they did not come close to doing him justice. He had been a television star, known for playing the tough but unforgettable Marine platoon sergeant on one of the funniest shows of its time. Standing there, I kept thinking the same thing: a life can be so much bigger than what a stone can say.

Later that same day, I was home working with my coin collection, using my phone to look up mintages and history. That is when it hit me. Why could I do that for a coin, but not for a headstone? Why could there not be a simple way to tap a chip and open the story behind a life, a place, a keepsake, or a memory?

From there, the ideas kept coming. Memorials. Auto restorations. Home remodeling. Collections. Family stories. Business displays. A chip placed in something meaningful, with the story attached to it forever. Not just information — connection.

But after the research, I realized the chips themselves did not have enough memory to hold everything I imagined. The stories needed a place to live. That meant either trusting somebody else’s platform, depending on the cloud, or building something of my own that would give people more control and privacy.

A friend suggested creating my own website. He would build the backend, and I would shape the frontend and the vision. Wanting more security and control, I left Microsoft Windows behind, installed Linux, and committed to building something that felt true to the idea from the very beginning.

And that is how MyStoryChip.com was born — not as social media, not as noise, and not as just another storage platform, but as a simple way to connect real-world objects to the stories, meaning, and memories behind them.
